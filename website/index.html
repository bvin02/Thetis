Why it matters

One hatchery spent a year chasing bacteria. It was the water.

The first answer isn’t always the right one. Thetis reads the larvae and the water together at every check, and says which pattern they match.

T-03 Critical Bacterial pattern T-04 At risk Corrosive water
  1. July 2007

    Larvae start dying at Whiskey Creek Shellfish Hatchery in Oregon, at a scale unseen in its 30 years. Managers put it down to a bloom of Vibrio bacteria and eliminate it. The larvae keep dying.

  2. Summer 2008

    Staff investigate seawater chemistry for the first time and record pH as low as 7.6 near the intakes. Adding sodium carbonate brings a marked improvement in survival and growth, too late to save the season.

  3. 2008 total

    About 25% of a normal season’s production.

  4. Since then

    Monitoring and treating incoming water has meant billions more oyster larvae for Pacific Northwest growers.

Source: Barton et al. 2015, Oceanography 28(2)

Questions

Something else? Ask us.

Do I need new hardware?

No. Thetis works from photos taken through the microscope you already own. It reads the scale from the counting grid in the photo, or you can set it by hand by drawing a line of known length.

Which species does it work with?

Thetis is built around oyster larvae, from D-stage to eyed. If you grow something else, tell us what and we’ll tell you how well it fits.

How accurate is the count?

It depends on your microscope and your photos, so we’d rather test it on yours than quote a number. Thetis marks the larvae it isn’t sure about, and a tech can correct any call. The counts update straight away.

Does Thetis dose tanks or control equipment?

No. Thetis recommends, and a person approves or dismisses each step. Nothing acts on equipment.

How are doses worked out?

From your latest pH, Ω aragonite, temperature and salinity, using the published seawater constants in the DOE best-practices guide (Dickson et al. 2007). You can choose sodium carbonate, sodium hydroxide or sodium bicarbonate. Doses are estimates, so the card says to try it in a jar first and re-measure 30 minutes after dosing.

Where does the husbandry advice come from?

Non-obvious guidance carries its source on the card. Most of it comes from the FAO’s Hatchery culture of bivalves manual (Helm and Bourne, 2004) and Barton et al. 2015.

What does it do without AI?

Counting, corrections, risk flags, doses, yield suggestions and forecasts all run without Claude. Claude adds the brief, findings across tanks, second opinions on unsure larvae and answers to your questions.

Where does my data go?

Thetis runs on a computer at your hatchery, and your records stay on it. When you use a Claude feature, the records or photo crops it needs go to Anthropic’s API. Leave Claude off and nothing leaves that computer.

Are the numbers on this page real?

No. Every screen here shows example data from a made-up hatchery, and the slide is a synthetic sample drawn by our own tools. The doses are calculated the same way the product calculates them, for that example water.
